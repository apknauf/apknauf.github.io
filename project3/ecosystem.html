Beyond the Animals

Understanding the Amazon Ecosystem

The Amazon is not just a collection of animals. It is a connected system of forests, rivers, wetlands, soils, plants, wildlife, and people.

Amazon at a Glance

Several features make the Amazon unusually important to study.

1 in 10

About one in ten known species on Earth are associated with the Amazon biome.

20%

The Amazon biome contains about 20% of the world's liquid freshwater according to WWF.

Millions

Millions of species live in the Amazon, including many that remain undescribed.

Many Communities

People across the region depend on forests and rivers for food, materials, livelihoods, and culture.

Layers of the Rainforest

Different heights create different conditions for plants and animals.

Emergent Layer

The tallest trees rise above the general canopy and experience strong sunlight, wind, and weather.

Canopy

The canopy forms a dense roof of leaves and branches where many birds, insects, and arboreal mammals live.

Understory

Filtered light, high humidity, and dense vegetation create a quieter environment below the canopy.

Forest Floor

Leaf litter, fungi, roots, decomposers, and ground-dwelling animals dominate the lowest layer.

Climate and Water

Warm temperatures and frequent rainfall support year-round plant growth. Water moves through the forest, atmosphere, rivers, wetlands, and soils as part of a connected cycle.

Amazon River Basin

The basin covers a huge area of South America and includes a network of rivers, floodplains, lakes, and seasonally inundated forests.

Biodiversity

Plants, fungi, insects, fish, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and mammals occupy specialized niches and depend on one another.

Environmental Threats

Conservation is an important part of understanding the Amazon.

Deforestation

Forest conversion can fragment habitat and reduce the continuity that wildlife needs.

Fires and Drought

Hotter, drier conditions can increase fire risk and place additional stress on forests.

Resource Extraction

Mining, logging, and other extraction can damage soil, water, and wildlife habitat.

Infrastructure

Roads and dams can alter landscapes and river connectivity when they are poorly planned.
